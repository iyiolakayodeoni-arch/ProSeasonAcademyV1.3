# 06 — Open Questions

Settled in the order they block things. Question 1 blocks enrolment; the rest do not.

---

## Blocking

### 1. What are we allowed to call ourselves?

The selling point leans on the word "university." That word is regulated in most African
countries, and there are real legal consequences for using it without authorisation.

This has to be answered before a single student is enrolled, because it determines what we can
say in public.

*Reduced urgency, slightly:* there is no certificate yet — it is gated on ten pros, see question 4
— so the immediate exposure is the name and the marketing, not a credential.

Options to evaluate:
- Register as something adjacent — an institute, an academy, a school of creative arts — and be
  explicit that we are university-*structured*, not a university.
- Pursue formal accreditation from day one (slow, expensive, but the strongest evidence).
- Partner with an accredited institution that co-signs the certificate.

**The principle that decides it:** we do not claim what we have not built. Whatever we choose, we
do not imply a status we do not hold.

*Status: unresolved. Needs legal advice for the specific country of first operation.*

### 2. ~~Which discipline first?~~ — SETTLED

**Esports, starting with EAFC 27, through the Preseason Academy.** Announced in

### 3. ~~Who is the first coach?~~ — SETTLED

**There is no coach in Season 1.** The season is solo by design — become the best you can be on
your own, and the lessons you produce become the material the **AI coach** in Season 2 is built
from. See [`14 — The Seasons Model`](./14-the-seasons-model.md).

This was previously logged as the hardest blocker. It is not a blocker — it was a wrong assumption
about the shape of the product.

### 3b. What are we actually allowed to call "open source"?

The pitch says the product is open source, then clarifies it means users decide which features get
built. The instinct is right but the term is technically specific, and misusing it costs credibility
with exactly the developers and partners we want.

Options: *built in the open*, *community-built*, *open roadmap*, or keep "open source" and accept
the correction from technical people. See
[`11 — Esports and the First Product`](./11-esports-and-the-first-product.md), section 5.

**Note:** whichever word is used, the feature-voting mechanism has to actually exist at launch.
The promise is in a video now.

## Not blocking, but decide soon

### 4. ~~What does the certificate certify?~~ — SETTLED

**It does not exist yet.** It unlocks only once Onliversity has helped at least ten people become
professionals and win certificates elsewhere. Until then Season 1 produces a public **record**.

See [`14 — The Seasons Model`](./14-the-seasons-model.md), section 4.

### 4b. What counts as "becoming a pro"?

The ten-pro gate is the strongest commitment in the project, and it is meaningless if the word
"pro" is soft. A signed team? Prize money above a threshold? An organiser's certification?

**And who certifies them?** If the answer is us, the gate proves nothing. It has to be external —
a tournament, a team, an organiser. That is the entire point of it.

### 4c. What is the Season 1 record called?

It is explicitly not a certificate, and it needs a name that does not imply one. Whatever it is
called, a player has to be able to leave Season 1 holding something.

### 5. Free, paid, or sponsored?

If the child "like me" is the reason this exists, cost is not a detail. The AI coach changes the
math — it scales without a salary — but infrastructure, tournaments and the people running the
community still cost something.

### 6. How does this sit alongside formal school?

Most of these students are in secondary school. Is Onliversity after-school, holiday, or parallel?
This determines the calendar, the workload, and the conversation with the parent.

### 7. What age range?

"Children" is doing a lot of work in the mission. A 10-year-old and a 17-year-old need different
structures — and the progression model in doc 02 (levels by work, not by age) needs a floor and a
ceiling to be workable.

### 8. How exactly are the students involved in building it?

"They are involved in the process" is the principle. The mechanism is not yet designed: a student
council, published decision logs, curriculum review cycles, credit for contributions. This needs
a real answer, not a slogan.

### 9. What is the community platform?

Pillar 2 lives or dies on it. It has to be somewhere students in Africa can actually reach daily,
on the devices they actually have, at a cost we can carry.

### 10. What are the exact colours and the character design?

Purple and black is decided — the specific palette is not. Neither is typography, and neither is
the founder's animated character, which needs to be drawn once, locked, and then reused everywhere.

There is also a trap worth naming: purple-and-black is the default esports palette. The palette is
ours; the *treatment* still has to be, or we look like every other gaming brand.

See [`09 — The Brand`](./09-the-brand.md), section 2.

### 11. Who makes the memes?

The brand is meme-native, which means it has to move at the speed of the culture and be made by
someone inside it. Principle 2 says the students should be involved rather than marketed at — but
the mechanism is not designed.

### 12. How does Founders Week actually work?

The user uses the product for a week and then decides the price. Unresolved: is that price binding,
suggested or advisory? Does it apply to every cohort or only the first? What happens to the people
who set it at zero?

Pay-what-you-want has known failure modes — anchoring, free-riding, and a low average that gets
read as low value. The mechanism is good; the details decide whether it survives contact.

### 13. What is Preseason Academy's calendar?

A preseason implies a season. What are the dates, how long is a cohort, and how does it fit beside
a Nigerian secondary school timetable? This is question 6, now made concrete and urgent.

### 14. Who is "Mumsi"?

The pitch opens with *"Omo, Mumsi dey actually vex oooo."* It reads as the mother from the original
video. If it is a name or a specific reference, the script needs it exact before shooting.

### 15. How long is Season 1, and what ends it?

A season without an ending is just an app. Dates, a closing event, and a defined finish line all
need to exist before anyone enrolls.

### 16. What does the AI coach need from Season 1's data?

The lessons captured in Season 1 are the training material for the Season 2 coach. If nobody
defines what shape that data has to be in, Season 2 has nothing to build on — and the capture
mechanism has to be designed *before* Season 1 starts, not after.
