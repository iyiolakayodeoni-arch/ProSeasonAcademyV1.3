# 11 — Esports and the First Product

Announced in [`10 — The Video Pitch`](./10-the-video-pitch.md):

> We are starting with **esports**, to give structure to esports players. The first game we are
> starting with is **EAFC 27**. And we are building **Preseason Academy** as the first product from
> Onliversity.

This settles open question 2 in [`06 — Open Questions`](./06-open-questions.md) — the first
discipline is esports.

---

## 1. Why esports is the right first discipline

The choice was not arbitrary, and it is worth being able to say why:

- **It is the creative career parents dismiss most completely.** Which makes it the purest test of
  the whole thesis. If the structure can make *this* legible, it can make anything legible.
- **The teenager already knows it is real.** No convincing required on their side. They watch the
  tournaments. They know players earn. The gap is only on the adult side — and that gap is exactly
  what the structure closes.
- **It already has a natural structure to borrow from.** Seasons, teams, coaches, leagues,
  transfers, stats, scouting. Esports invented its own version of the university's four pillars
  years ago; we are formalising it, not inventing it.
- **The work is instantly verifiable.** A match result cannot be argued with. That is a portfolio
  that grades itself — the strongest possible form of
  [`03`](./03-the-evidence-strategy.md)'s *we show, we do not argue*.
- **It is continental.** Playable across Africa on hardware people already own, with an audience
  that is already there.

## 2. The four pillars, mapped to esports

From [`02 — The University Blueprint`](./02-the-university-blueprint.md). Esports already invented
its own version of all four — but they arrive in stages, not at once. See
[`14 — The Seasons Model`](./14-the-seasons-model.md).

| Pillar | University | Esports at Onliversity | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Someone is teaching it** | A professor | An **AI coach** — does the research, watches your matches, works with you day to day as a pro | **Season 2** |
| **People are learning with them** | A class | A **squad / cohort** that trains together, scrimmages together, reviews together | Season 1 |
| **There is a cert after** | A degree | A **public record** — matches, results, rank progression, VODs, lessons. The **certificate** only at ten pros. | Record: Season 1 · Cert: ten pros |
| **There is a structure** | Levels and a syllabus | A **season** with a preseason, a competitive phase, and a finish line. Levels by performance, not by age. | Season 1 |

**Preseason Academy is pillar 4 with a name on it.** The metaphor is not decorative — a preseason
is genuinely the structured preparation phase before competition, which is precisely what an
academy is for. It is the strongest naming instinct in the whole product.

**Season 1 has no coach and no certificate, and that is the design.** Solo journey first; the
lessons it produces are what the AI coach is built from. Both gaps get published before anyone
enrolls.

## 3. Preseason Academy

The first product from Onliversity.

What Season 1 has to be — with no coach and no credential carrying it:

- A **cohort**, capped, enrolled together, training on a schedule
- A **curriculum** with levels and a finish line — what you can do at each level, explicitly
- **The ladder and VOD review** as the assessment — the season grades you, not a person
- A **public record** that accumulates — and is explicitly not called a certificate
- A **community space** the cohort uses daily, carrying the room since there is no coach in it
- A **lesson-capture mechanism** — Season 1's lessons are Season 2's training data
- A **public ten-pro counter** everyone can watch move

The minimum viable structure checklist in
[`02 — The University Blueprint`](./02-the-university-blueprint.md), section 3, is the full list.

## 4. Founders Week — the pricing

From the pitch:

> You get to use the product for one week — which is called the **Founders Week** — where you use
> it and get to decide what the price is, in real time. So the price is free and fair, and not
> hardcoded by the organisation.

And the reasoning behind it:

> The price be the problem, no be the value. Hand dey meet everybody.

This is a genuinely good mechanism and it is worth being precise about why:

- **It removes the only real objection.** The pitch correctly identifies that the barrier is not
  doubt about the value, it is money. So it takes money off the table for a week.
- **It is honest.** A free trial hides the price. Founders Week makes the user set it, which turns
  a sales tactic into a piece of evidence about what the product is worth.
- **It generates the best data available.** What people choose to pay, unprompted, is the truest
  pricing research that exists — and it can be published, which turns pricing into more proof.
- **It is on-brand.** Built by the people, priced by the people. Consistent with principle 2.

**Design questions this raises** (all logged in doc 06): pay-what-you-want has known failure modes —
anchoring, free-riding, and the risk that a low average gets read as low value. Whether the chosen
price is binding, suggested, or advisory has not been decided.

## 5. "Open source" — say it precisely

From the pitch:

> This is open source — not in the sense that you can fork features, more of: you get to decide the
> new features that are being added in the application. So it's built by the people, in a way.

The instinct is right and the founder already caught the nuance in real time. Naming it precisely
matters, because "open source" has a specific technical meaning and misusing it will cost
credibility with exactly the developers and partners we want.

| | |
|---|---|
| **What we mean** | Open **roadmap** and open **direction**. Users propose and vote on what gets built next. The product is shaped by the people using it. |
| **What we do not mean** | A public codebase under an open-source licence that anyone can fork. |
| **Better words to consider** | *Built in the open.* *Community-built.* *Open roadmap.* *You decide what we build next.* |

This is principle 6 — *we do not claim what we have not built* — applied to vocabulary. It is also
principle 2 made real: **students are co-builders, not customers.** A published feature vote with
visible results is the mechanism that makes that sentence true instead of a slogan.

**The commitment is now public.** Once it is in a video, the feature-voting has to actually exist
at launch, or the first thing the community notices is that the promise was a line in a script.

## 6. Onliversity.app

The single destination from the pitch. Everything routes there.

For the video to work, the site has to deliver on one specific line: *"the details of the product,
everything is there."* That means, at minimum:

- What Preseason Academy actually is — the curriculum, the levels, the calendar
- What Season 1 is, what Season 2 is, and exactly what does not exist yet
- The public ten-pro counter
- What Founders Week is and how the pricing works
- The feature-vote mechanism, live
- The signup

If the video says everything is there and it is not, the pitch has broken
[`04 — Principles`](./04-principles.md), principle 6 on launch day.
