## 1. The idea, and why it works

There is an existing video: a young man's creativity gets shut down because it is not science. It
currently just… ends. On the dejection.

**The move: that video was never a standalone video. It was a publicity stunt for Onliversity.**

The new video continues the plot, and the continuation *is* the reveal — we cut from the young
man's story into a press conference where the founder of Onliversity is addressing exactly the
problem we just watched.

Why this is strong:

- **The plot gets completed.** The dejection does not hang unresolved; it gets answered. That is
  emotionally satisfying in a way a straight ad never is.
- **The viewer was recruited.** They watched the problem believing it was a story. Discovering it
  was a setup makes them a participant, not an audience. That is the shareable moment.
- **It demonstrates the argument.** We are not *saying* creative work has structure — the video
  itself is structured creative work, made by the people saying it.
- **It is re-watchable.** The first video changes meaning after the second. People go back.

## 2. Structure

| | Beat | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Continuity** | Pick up the existing video. The young man, dejected. His mother's fear is still in the room. Let it sit — do not resolve it yet. |
| **2** | **The reveal** | The turn. That video was a setup — a publicity stunt for Onliversity. |
| **3** | **The press conference** | Cut to the founder's animated character at a podium. A room full of people. Investors, press, and ordinary viewers alike. |
| **4** | **The pitch** | The script below. Hook → empathy → product → objection → CTA. |
| **5** | **The questions** | The room asks the questions a teenager would actually ask. The character answers them. |

Beat 5 is the thing that turns this from an ad into the product's own demonstration: teenagers get
to watch their exact doubts being asked out loud and answered honestly.

## 3. The pitch script

Delivered by the animated founder character. Pidgin stays exactly where it is.

---

### BEAT A — The hook

> Do you sometimes feel like the young man in this video? Like your talents and creativity are
> always seen as the backup plan — and not the main career, or the main thing?
>
> Well, I have felt the same way as well. Which is why I am building Onliversity — the digital
> university for creatives.

### BEAT B — Concede the other side first

> Omo, Mumsi dey actually vex oooo.
>
> My people, I actually have a startup product that solves this woman's fears. She is afraid that
> her son's future is ruined if he doesn't go to uni — **and she is right, to be honest.** With the
> way the country is, uni increases your chance of getting a good job and actually doing something
> for yourself.

*Do not soften this and do not skip it. Granting the mother's fear before offering the alternative
is the most persuasive thing in the entire pitch. Arguing with her is the version that loses.*

### BEAT C — The alternative, not a dream

> I am not here to sell a dream to you. I am here to provide an alternative.
>
> Wouldn't you like to live in a world where your creative passion no longer becomes a passion —
> but is a real career that is respected, and people do not bang the door on your accomplishment?
>
> That is the problem my startup solves. The name is Onliversity — a digital university for
> creatives, giving you the structure that people mock us for lacking.

### BEAT D — The product, the part they care about

> We are starting with **esports**, to give structure to esports players. The first game we are
> starting with is **EAFC 27**. And we are building **Preseason Academy** as the first product from
> Onliversity.

*Note: the original instinct was "the parts they care about first, sha" — and this is that part.
For a teenager, this beat is the actual news. Everything before it earns the right to say this;
this is why they keep watching. Consider pulling it earlier if the room tests slow.*

### BEAT E — The objection, named before it lands

> This sounds all good and fine, but I know what you are thinking: another startup, another
> subscription that I didn't even plan for.
>
> Relax. You get to use the product for one week — which is called the **Founders Week** — where
> you use it and get to decide what the price is, in real time. So the price is free and fair, and
> not hardcoded by the organisation.

### BEAT F — Built by the people

> So this is open source — not in the sense that you can fork features, more of: you get to decide
> the new features that are being added in the application. So it's built by the people, in a way.

*Careful beat — this is a real promise with a real cost. It maps directly to principle 2 in
[`04 — Principles`](./04-principles.md): students are co-builders, not customers. Once said out
loud in a video, it has to be true. See the note in
[`11 — Esports and the First Product`](./11-esports-and-the-first-product.md).*

### BEAT G — The close

> Enough of my yapping. Go to **Onliversity.app** — there you can get more information on
> everything you need to know, the details of the product, everything is there.
>
> I am not saying the product details, because I know all of you — nah, the price be the problem,
> no be the value. Hand dey meet everybody, I know. We understand that, and we have put that into
> consideration.
>
> So don't forget — click the link and sign up. Let's build this university for creatives together.

## 4. Beat 5 — the questions

The room asks what a teenager would ask. Directly from the objection table in
[`03 — The Evidence Strategy`](./03-the-evidence-strategy.md), section 5:

| The question | What the answer has to be |
|---|---|
| "Is this actually real, or just another online thing?" | The structure, shown: cohort, levels, ladder, finish line — plus a published list of what does not exist yet. |
| "I'm not good enough for this." | Levels by the work, not by talent. A first result on the ladder in week one. |
| "Who is teaching this?" | **"Nobody yet."** Season 1 is solo by design. The AI coach arrives in Season 2, built from what Season 1 learns. |
| "Nobody will take it seriously." | A public record that exists whether or not anyone believes in you — and a certificate that unlocks only at ten pros. |
| "I don't have time, school is eating me." | A calendar that fits beside school. Still open — doc 06, question 6. |
| "How much is it?" | Founders Week. You set the price. |
| "My parents will say no." | Not argued. The structure gives them something to point at; the results come later. |

Every answer here must be **true at the time of release.** The video is a claim, and
principle 6 — *we do not claim what we have not built* — applies to a script too.

## 5. Production notes

- **The founder's face does not appear.** The animated character carries every scene. See
  [`09 — The Brand`](./09-the-brand.md), section 3.
- **Purple and black throughout.** The press conference should not look like a corporate press
  conference — it should look like ours.
- **The character's design must be locked before shooting.** One asset, reused.
- **Beat 2 is the whole video.** If the reveal does not land, the rest is just an ad. Cut it tight
  and test it on someone who has not seen the plan.
- **Meme language belongs in the edit,** not the script — captions, cuts, reaction frames.

## 6. One tension to be aware of

The pitch is explicitly for "investors and people alike," and it spends real time on the mother's
fears. That is deliberate and it works — but note that [`03 — The Evidence Strategy`](./03-the-evidence-strategy.md)
says the parent is a *byproduct, not a target*.

These are consistent, and the distinction is worth keeping sharp: **the video acknowledges the
parent; it does not argue with them.** Beat B grants the mother's fear and moves on. It never tries
to persuade her. The moment a version of this script starts arguing that she is wrong, it has
broken both the pitch and the strategy.

## 7. Transcription fixes

The raw source is speech-to-text. These are the only changes made, all obvious slips:

| Raw | Here |
|---|---|
| "leave in a world" | "live in a world" |
| "what our product those" | "what our product does" |
| "she is afraid that her sons future is ruined i he doesn't go to uni" | "her son's future is ruined if he doesn't go to uni" |
| "am not here to sell" | "I am not here to sell" |
| "i he doesn't" | "if he doesn't" |

**Nothing else was altered.** The Pidgin is untouched: *"Omo, Mumsi dey actually vex oooo"*,
*"nah, the price be the problem, no be the value"*, *"hand dey meet everybody"*, *"enough of my
yapping."* Those are the voice — see [`09 — The Brand`](./09-the-brand.md), section 4.

**One thing to confirm before shooting:** who or what "Mumsi" refers to. It reads as the mother in
the original video. If it is a name or a specific reference, the script needs it exact.
