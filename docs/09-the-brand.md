# 09 — The Brand

The brand is not decoration on top of the structure. For this audience it **is** the first piece of
evidence. A teenager decides in about three seconds whether this thing was made for them or made
*at* them, and no amount of curriculum survives that first impression being wrong.

The bar, stated plainly: **the viewer has to feel seen and heard.** Gen Z, African, unmistakably.

---

## 1. The test everything gets held to

Before anything ships — a colour, a post, a line of copy, a video frame — one question:

> **Would a Nigerian teenager screenshot this and send it to their friend?**

If no, it is not done. This is the same discipline as
[`04 — Principles`](./04-principles.md), principle 5 — *we show, we do not argue* — applied to the
brand. A screenshot sent to a friend is an artefact. A brand guideline document is not.

## 2. Visual identity

| | Decision | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Colours** | **Purple and black.** | Stated, decided. Purple is the differentiator — most education branding is blue, and blue reads institutional. Purple against black reads like a game, a poster, an album. |
| **Feel** | Different. Not corporate, not school. | The goal is that it does not look like anything a parent would forward. |
| **Imagery** | **Memes.** Current trends. | Memes are not filler content here — they are the native language of the audience. Using them badly is worse than not using them. See section 5. |
| **Audience the visuals serve** | Teenagers and young adults. | Not children, not professionals. The design should look like it belongs on their phone, not in a prospectus. |

**What purple and black has to avoid:** the default "esports purple neon gradient" that every
gaming brand already uses. Purple and black is the palette; the *treatment* still has to be ours.
Open question in [`06`](./06-open-questions.md).

## 3. The founder character

The founder appears in the brand and the video as **an animated character that looks like him —
not his actual face.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **What it is** | An animated stand-in, recognisably the founder, stylised. |
| **What it is not** | His face. Not a deepfake, not a photorealistic avatar, not a faceless logo mascot. |
| **Why animated** | It is shareable, remixable and meme-able in a way a real face is not. It can be put in situations, drawn in reactions, reused in clips. It also keeps the founder's privacy intact while still giving the brand a *person*. |
| **Why a person at all** | Principle 1: someone is teaching it. A brand with a face is a brand someone can trust. A logo cannot be accountable to a cohort. |

The character is an asset. It should be designed once, properly, and then reused everywhere — video,
memes, the app, stickers. Consistency is what makes it read as a character instead of a drawing.

## 4. Voice

The founder's own pitch in [`08 — Source Pitch`](./08-source-pitch.md) is the voice. It does not
need changing, it needs protecting.

What it does that works, and should be kept deliberately:

- **Code-switching without apology.** *"Omo, Mumsi dey actually vex oooo."* English and Pidgin in
  the same breath, the way people actually talk. Translating it into formal English kills it.
- **Self-awareness.** *"Enough of my yapping."* The brand knows it is talking. It does not perform
  sincerity.
- **Conceding the other side first.** *"She is right, to be honest."* The mother's fear is granted
  before the alternative is offered. This is the single most persuasive move in the whole pitch and
  it must survive into the script.
- **Naming the objection before it is raised.** *"I know what you are thinking: another startup,
  another subscription."*
- **Refusing to oversell.** *"I am not here to sell a dream to you. I am here to provide an
  alternative."*

**The rule:** never write a line for the brand that the founder would not say out loud to a friend.

## 5. Memes — the risk

Memes are the highest-value, highest-risk part of this brand.

- **Right:** memes that a teenager would make themselves, posted at the speed the culture moves.
- **Wrong:** a brand account explaining the joke, or posting a format three weeks late, or using
  meme language in a corporate sentence. This is the fastest way to be labelled as an outsider, and
  once labelled, the teenager is gone.

Practical consequence of principle 2 in [`04`](./04-principles.md) — *students are co-builders, not
customers*: **the memes should be made with the students, not at them.** Whoever runs this needs to
be inside the culture already, or hand it to someone who is.

## 6. Why this brand strategy is structurally correct

This is not a marketing decision sitting next to the mission. It is the same argument:

The teenager has been told their thing is unserious. A brand that looks like a school confirms it.
A brand that looks like *them* — purple and black, memes, a character who talks like their friend —
is the first structural signal that says **this was built for you by people who are you.**

Which is exactly what the mission in [`01 — The Core Thinking`](./01-core-thinking.md) promises.
