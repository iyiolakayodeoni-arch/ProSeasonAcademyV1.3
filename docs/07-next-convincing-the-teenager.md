## What has since been settled

The answer to "how does the teenager come to believe this is worth it" now has a shape:

- **They have to feel seen and heard.** Gen Z, African, unmistakably. That is the bar, and it is
  the thing that actually convinces them — not the argument. See
  [`09 — The Brand`](./09-the-brand.md).
- **The person saying it is a character, not a suit.** The founder as an animated character who
  looks like him and talks like them. See
  [`09 — The Brand`](./09-the-brand.md), section 3.
- **The first thing they see is a video that completes a story they already watched** — and reveals
  it was a setup. See [`10 — The Video Pitch`](./10-the-video-pitch.md).
- **The answer to "how much" is Founders Week** — use it for a week, you set the price. See
  [`11 — Esports and the First Product`](./11-esports-and-the-first-product.md), section 4.
- **The answer to "is this real" is the structure**, shown in the video's question segment —
  cohort, levels, ladder, finish line, and a published list of what does not exist yet. There is no
  coach in Season 1 and no certificate until ten pros; saying so is part of the answer, not a
  weakness in it. See [`10`](./10-the-video-pitch.md), section 4 and
  [`14 — The Seasons Model`](./14-the-seasons-model.md).

## The decision that put this here

Trying to convince the parents is a waste of time. The argument is unwinnable, it is unnecessary
because the scale moves them anyway, and effort spent there is effort spent losing.

So the only audience that matters right now is **the teenager** — and the only question that
matters is:

> **How do they come to believe this programme is worth it?**

That is the next piece of work. Not started.

## The open questions

1. **Where are they?** — *still open.* Which platforms, communities and spaces the teenager is
   already in. Esports narrows this a long way; it has not been written down yet.
2. **~~Who says it?~~** — *answered.* The founder, as an animated character. Noted tension worth
   keeping in mind: a teenager who already went through the programme is still a stronger voice
   than the founder, and from cohort two onwards they should be the ones talking.
3. **~~What is the first proof they touch?~~** — *answered.* The video and its reveal, then
   Onliversity.app.
4. **What do they get in week one?** — *still open, and now urgent.* Something finished, fast. The
   strongest answer to "I'm not good enough" is a result that exists. In EAFC 27 terms this is
   concrete and should be easy to design.
5. **~~What is the ask?~~** — *answered.* Founders Week: use it a week, set the price. Mechanics
   still open — doc 06, question 12.
6. **What do they tell their friends?** — *still open.* The reveal is a strong candidate: *"that
   video was actually an ad, and it worked on me."* It has not been tested.

## What this is not

This is not a marketing plan, and it does not become one. The programme's hold on a teenager comes
from the structure being real, not from the messaging being good. Whatever gets designed here has
to be true — the same rule as everywhere else in this repo.
