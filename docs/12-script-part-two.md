## ⚠ Read this before you animate — the name problem

Part 1 is branded **ProCoach** — *"the ProCoach waitlist video."* Part 2 sells **Onliversity** and
its first product **Preseason Academy**.

If Part 2 just says a new name, the audience reads it as a different company entirely, and the
continuation loses its power. This script solves it in **Shot 12** with one line that retcons the
relationship:

> *"The name is Onliversity — a digital university for creatives. And the first product out of it
> is the one you saw a glimpse of: Preseason Academy."*

**You have two choices, and you need to pick one before animating:**

| Option | What you do | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Preseason Academy wins** | Keep the line as written. ProCoach becomes a working title that got renamed. | One line of dialogue. Cheapest. |
| **B — ProCoach wins** | Replace "Preseason Academy" with "ProCoach" throughout and re-edit Part 1's end card. | Part 1 needs a re-render, and you lose the better name. |

Recommendation: **A.** "Preseason Academy" is stronger, and the cost is a single line.

## THE SCRIPT

### INT. GAMING ROOM — NIGHT (CONTINUOUS)

**SHOT 8 — 0:31–0:38** *(7s, no dialogue)*
Fade up from the black, same room, seconds later. The RGB is still on but it reads colder now.

### THE REVEAL

**SHOT 10 — 0:41–0:48** *(7s, no dialogue)*
**The shot that makes the whole video.** Push *into* the phone screen and keep going — through it —
and emerge on the other side into a completely different space: a press conference. Long lens,
shallow depth, camera bodies in the foreground silhouetted.

The implication lands without a word of explanation: **the story you just watched was a setup.**

**SHOT 11 — 0:48–0:56** *(8s, no dialogue)*
Wide. A podium, dead centre, black with a purple Onliversity mark. A backdrop of cameras and
phones — some of them held by teenagers in the crowd, recording. Reporters seated.

At the podium: **the FOUNDER** — animated character, stylised, recognisably you. Confident. Not
nervous. He has been waiting for this question.

**ON SCREEN (lower third):** `IYIOLA — FOUNDER, ONLIVERSITY`

He adjusts the mic. Looks straight down the lens — at the viewer, not the room.

## CONTINUITY NOTES

1. **Part 1 ended on black at 0:31.** Shot 8 fades up from it — do not re-use the door-close shot as
   the opening, it will read as a mistake. The one-frame flashback belongs in Shot 12.
2. **The RGB lighting has to change temperature.** Warm in Part 1, colder in Shot 8, replaced
   entirely by purple once the phone lights up. That colour shift *is* the story.
3. **The founder character must be locked before Shot 9.** One design, reused in every subsequent
   asset — the app, the memes, the stickers. See [09 — The Brand](./09-the-brand.md), section 3.
4. **Every claim in this script has to be true on release day.** Founders Week, the feature vote,
   the product page. Principle 6: *we do not claim what we have not built.* The video is a promise
   with a publish date.
   **The script is clean on this** — it never says "coach" or "certificate" out loud. Keep it that
   way. It sells the structure, the season and Founders Week, all of which exist in Season 1. If
   anyone adds a line about a coach or a credential, cut it.
5. **"Mummy" vs "Mumsi."** The raw pitch said *"Mumsi dey actually vex."* I read that as *"Mummy"* —
   it scans correctly and matches the mother in Part 1. **If Mumsi is a name or a specific
   reference, put it back before recording.**

## PART 3 — what Shot 20 sets up

The boy has the phone and he is smiling. Part 3 is his first week — Founders Week, from the inside.
Which means Part 3 can only be made once the product exists, because it should be real footage of a
real first cohort, not animation.

That is the sequence working as intended: **Part 1 is the problem, Part 2 is the promise, Part 3 is
the proof.** See [03 — The Evidence Strategy](./03-the-evidence-strategy.md).
