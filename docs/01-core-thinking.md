# 01 — The Core Thinking

**Onliversity in one line:** a university structure for creatives in Africa — so that the ones
who were told their dreams don't count get the same scaffolding everybody else got.

---

## 1. The problem, stated plainly

There is a child who is like me. They can draw, or write, or sing, or build, or design, or film.
And somewhere along the line they learned a second thing: *that this does not count.*

Not because anyone sat them down and said it. Because of what the world around them structures.
Nobody builds a path for it. Nobody enrols them. Nobody examines them. Nobody hands them anything
at the end. They are told to treat it as a hobby, and to focus on the thing that has a path.

So the child concludes — reasonably, from the evidence available to them — that they don't matter
and the dream isn't valid.

**The wound is not a lack of talent. It is a lack of structure.**

## 2. The diagnosis

The belief that creative careers are not important is real, and it is load-bearing. It sits under
how parents spend money, how schools allocate timetables, how relatives talk at gatherings.

But here is the part that matters: **that belief was not built by argument. It was built by
infrastructure.** Medicine has faculties, lecture halls, deans, clinical years, a certificate on
the wall, a title after your name. The creative path has none of that, so it reads as unserious —
even to the child doing it.

Belief follows structure. Which means you do not defeat the belief by attacking it. You build the
structure, and the belief moves on its own.

That is the whole strategy. Everything else in this repo is detail.

## 3. What universities actually have (the question that started this)

Asked directly: *what structure do those universities have that makes them work well?*

The honest answer is short. It is not prestige, and it is not buildings.

| | The structure | What it does for the child |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Someone is teaching it** | The craft is being transmitted by a person who has it. It is not self-taught in a vacuum. |
| 2 | **People are learning with them** | A community behind them. They are not alone in it. There are peers, and the peers are real. |
| 3 | **There is a cert after** | It ends in something. The years are not just spent — they are *banked*. |
| 4 | **There is a structure at all** | Levels, progression, expectations, a beginning and an end. It is a thing you *do*, not a thing you *have*. |

Everything else a university has — the campus, the dean, the matriculation, the alumni network —
is elaboration on those four. So those four are what we build. Full treatment in
[`02-the-university-blueprint.md`](./02-the-university-blueprint.md).

## 4. Our position on the sciences

We are **not** against the sciences. Not competing with them, not replacing them, not making a
child choose.

We are saying something narrower and much harder to argue with: *creative work also produces real
value, and it also deserves structure.*

Two reasons this framing is deliberate:

- **It is true.** A war on the sciences is unwinnable and we would lose the parents instantly.
- **It is unnecessary.** We do not need anyone to love physics less. We need them to see what
  happens when a creative child is finally given a path, teachers, a cohort and a certificate.

The sciences keep their position. We are building the seat next to it that was never built.

## 5. The selling point

> **The same structure a university has — but better, and way more suited to creative careers.**

Two things to notice about that sentence, because they carry the weight:

**"The same structure"** is the credibility claim. It is what makes the thing legible to a parent
who has never taken a creative career seriously. We are not asking them to imagine a new category.
We are pointing at a category they already respect and saying: *this child is enrolled in it.*

**"Better and way more suited"** is the differentiator — and it is *better for this child*, not
better than university in the abstract. Lectures teach you to recall; a studio teaches you to
ship. A transcript lists courses you sat; a portfolio shows what you can actually make. An
exam hall measures memory under pressure; a critique measures the work.

Keep the claim precise. "Better suited to creative careers" is defensible and specific.
"Better than university" is a boast, and boasts lose parents.

## 6. The proof strategy

The hardest truth in all of this:

> I can't change their minds without evidence — especially the parents. Nothing I say would.

So the plan is **not** a persuasion plan. It is an evidence plan, and it runs in two tracks at
once:

**Track one — structural parity (now, day one).** We cannot show scale yet. But we can show,
today, that the structure exists: there is a cohort, there is a curriculum, there is a ladder, and
there is a record. The parity table in doc 02 *is* the argument. It does not require the child to
succeed first — and it now includes a published list of what does *not* exist yet, with the season
each piece arrives in. See [`14-the-seasons-model.md`](./14-the-seasons-model.md).

**Track two — scale (over time).** The real mind-changer. Parents will not be moved by an essay.
They will be moved by Onliversity being undeniably, visibly, unarguably big across Africa —
because then it stops being one person's idea and becomes a fact about the continent.

Track one gets us the first cohort. Track two changes the culture. Full treatment in
[`03-the-evidence-strategy.md`](./03-the-evidence-strategy.md).

### Who we are actually trying to convince

Not the parents. Trying to convince the parents is a waste of time — the argument is unwinnable,
and unnecessary, because the scale moves them without us asking.

**The audience is the teenager.** They are the only person who can start, and the only question
that matters right now is whether they come to believe this programme is worth it. The parent is a
byproduct, deferred — and how we reach the teenager is the next piece of work, deliberately left
open in [`07-next-convincing-the-teenager.md`](./07-next-convincing-the-teenager.md).

## 7. Where this is going

Online now. **Physical eventually, inshallah.**

The physical campus is not a vanity goal and not an afterthought. It is the final piece of
evidence — the thing that makes the whole project impossible to dismiss. A building is a
statement in a way a website never is.

But the order is strict: the structure gets proven online, at scale, first. Then it becomes
physical. Not before.

## 8. Whose project this is

Ours. The students'.

They are involved in the process. Nothing is hidden from them. Everything is transparent.

This is not a nice-to-have or a branding choice. It is load-bearing for three reasons:

- **It is honest.** We are asking a child to trust a structure that does not exist yet. The only
  way to earn that is to not hide anything.
- **It is structural.** Real universities are built by the people in them. If the students are
  just customers, we have built a course, not a university.
- **It is the point.** The entire promise is *you matter*. A place that says "you matter" while
  making every decision about you behind a closed door is lying.

Full treatment in [`04-principles.md`](./04-principles.md).
