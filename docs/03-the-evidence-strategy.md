# 03 — The Evidence Strategy

The central admission of this whole project:

> I can't change their minds without evidence — especially the parents. Their minds would be
> changed once they see how big Onliversity gets in Africa. That's the only way. Nothing I say
> would.

And the decision that follows from it:

> **Trying to convince the parents is a waste of time.** Convince the child — the teenager — that
> the programme is worth it. The parent question is downstream, and it is tomorrow's problem.

So this is not a persuasion document. It is a plan for manufacturing proof.

---

## 1. Who we are actually talking to

There is **one audience right now: the teenager.**

| | Who they are | Our posture |
|---|---|---|
| **The audience** | The child, the teenager | **The only one we are actively working on.** They need to believe the programme is worth their time. How we do that is still open — see [`07-next-convincing-the-teenager.md`](./07-next-convincing-the-teenager.md). |
| **The byproduct** | The parent | **Explicitly deferred.** Not an enemy, not a target — simply not our work. They move when the scale moves, and we do not spend effort on them directly. |
| **The amplifier** | Relatives, teachers, church, neighbours | Not targeted either. They follow the visible results. |
| **The enablers** | Partners, funders, collaborators | Recruited when needed. They need a credible plan, not a pitch. |

### Why the parent is deferred, and not just low priority

This was stated as a waste of time, and the reasoning holds up:

- **The argument is unwinnable.** "Nothing I say would" change their minds. Effort spent there is
  effort spent losing.
- **It is unnecessary.** The parent's mind changes as a *side effect* of Onliversity becoming big
  across Africa. We do not have to cause it. We have to cause the scale.
- **It is the wrong order.** You cannot show a parent outcomes before there are students, and you
  cannot get students by winning parents first. The teenager comes first because the teenager is
  the only one who can start.
- **It corrupts the product.** Build for the parent and you build something respectable. Build for
  the teenager and you build something they actually want.

**What we do for the parent instead:** we keep the structure inspectable — a curriculum they can
read, a ladder they can check, a record they can see, and a published list of what is not built
yet. That costs
nothing extra, because those things exist for the student anyway. When the parent eventually looks,
the evidence is already there. But we do not go and make them look.

## 2. The two tracks

### Track one — structural parity (now, aimed at the teenager)

We cannot show scale today. But scale is not the only evidence. On day one the structure can be
shown to exist, and to mirror the thing the teenager already knows is serious:

- There is a **cohort** — a real group, enrolled together, working together
- There is a **curriculum** — levels, a syllabus, a finish line
- There is a **ladder** — a real competitive record, ranked, public
- There is a **record** — matches, results, rank progression, VODs, lessons
- There is a **published gap** — what does not exist yet, and which season it arrives in

Note what is *not* on that list: a teacher and a certificate. Neither exists in Season 1, and
saying so is part of the evidence. A teenager who has been sold to before believes the gap list
more than the feature list. See [`14 — The Seasons Model`](./14-the-seasons-model.md).

The parity table in [`02 — The University Blueprint`](./02-the-university-blueprint.md) *is* this
argument. It requires no future success to be true. It is true today.

For a teenager who has been told their dream is a hobby, this is the part that lands: **someone
built the same machinery for you that they built for the doctors.**

**Track one gets us the first cohort.**

### Track two — scale (built over time, changes the parents without us asking)

Onliversity becoming undeniably, visibly, unarguably big across Africa — at which point it stops
being one person's idea and becomes a fact about the continent.

This is the track that moves the parents, and the reason we never have to argue with one. It is
also the slowest, which is exactly why it is not today's job.

**Track two changes the culture. We do not run it — we cause it.**

The order matters. Track one is the door. Track two is what closes it behind us.

## 3. The evidence ladder

Evidence that costs nothing to produce, moving up to evidence that cannot be argued with.

**Rung 1 — Existence.** The structure is there and can be inspected. Curriculum published. Ladder
live. Gaps published with a season attached to each. *Available immediately. Aimed at the teenager.*

**Rung 2 — Work.** Real student portfolios. Public. Dated. Verifiable by anyone. The single most
powerful artefact we will ever produce — and note that it convinces the teenager first, because the
teenager is the one who made it.

**Rung 3 — Numbers.** Enrolment. Completion. Retention. Cohorts run. Countries reached. Published
openly, including the unflattering ones.

**Rung 4 — Outcomes.** Where the graduates went. First paid work. Clients. Commissions. Stories
with names attached.

**Rung 5 — Presence.** Physical. Inshallah. The rung nobody argues with.

Build the rungs in order. Rung 5 without rungs 1–4 is a building with nothing in it.

## 4. What we measure

If we cannot count it, we cannot use it as evidence. The core set:

| Metric | Why it is evidence |
|---|---|
| Students enrolled | Reach — "how big is this getting" |
| Countries and states reached | That it is African, not one city |
| Completion rate | That this is a real programme people finish, not a signup list |
| Portfolios shipped | That real work exists at the end |
| Coaches active (from Season 2) | That there really is something teaching it |
| Graduates with paid work or clients | The outcome that eventually speaks for itself |
| Time from enrolment to first finished piece | That the structure accelerates rather than stalls |

Everything on this list gets published. See [`04 — Principles`](./04-principles.md), principle 5.

## 5. The objections, and the evidence that answers each

### From the teenager — this is the table that matters

| What they are actually thinking | The only answer that works |
|---|---|
| "Is this actually real, or just another online thing?" | The structure, visible: a cohort, levels, a ladder, a finish line. And a published list of what does not exist yet. |
| "I'm not good enough for this." | Levels based on the work, not on talent. A first result on the ladder in week one. |
| "Nobody will take it seriously." | A public record that exists whether or not anyone believes in you — and a certificate that unlocks at ten pros, so it can never be handed out lightly. |
| "I don't have time — school is eating me." | A calendar that fits beside school, not on top of it. Open question 6 in doc 06. |
| "My parents will say no." | Not our argument to make — and not the teenager's either, yet. The structure gives them something to point at, and the results come later. |
| "It's free, so it's worth nothing." | Work required, critique held, finish line enforced. Cost is open question 5 in doc 06. |

*How we actually reach and convince the teenager is the next piece of work — deliberately left
open. See [`07 — Next: Convincing the Teenager`](./07-next-convincing-the-teenager.md).*

### From the parent — answered passively, never argued

| The objection | The artefact that sits there when they look |
|---|---|
| "Creative work is not a real career." | A graduate with paying clients. Not an essay. |
| "This is not a real institution." | The parity table, plus an inspectable curriculum and named faculty. |
| "Who is teaching this?" | "Nobody yet. Season 1 is solo — that is the point. The AI coach arrives in Season 2, built from what Season 1 learns." |
| "Is it big? Is anyone actually in it?" | Published enrolment numbers, by country, updated openly. |
| "My child will fall behind." | Completion rates and portfolios. |
| "It's online, so it's not serious." | The cohort, the ladder, the finish line — and eventually the campus. |

Notice the pattern across both tables: **not one of these is answered with an argument.** Every
single one is answered with an artefact. That is the discipline.

## 6. Rules of engagement

1. **Never argue with a parent about the value of creative work.** Unwinnable and unnecessary. It
   is also not our job — the scale does it.
2. **Never exaggerate a number.** One inflated figure, caught, destroys the entire project — and
   the project's whole claim is that it is serious.
3. **Publish the failures too.** A completion rate that dips and is published anyway is worth more
   than a perfect number nobody believes.
4. **Let the work speak first.** The portfolio is the loudest thing we have. Lead with it.
5. **Do not attack the sciences, ever.** We are building the seat next to it, not arguing for the
   other one to be removed.
6. **Do not build for the parent.** Every decision gets tested against "does this make the teenager
   want to be here?" If the honest answer is "it makes us look respectable to adults," cut it.
